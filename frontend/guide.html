Deaf Culture & Etiquette

ASL is more than just hand signs; it's a rich culture. Here is how to be a respectful communicator.

The Golden Rules

👀
Maintain Eye Contact

Looking away is considered rude. In Deaf culture, eye contact signifies that you are listening.

📢
Don't Shout

Shouting doesn't help most deaf people hear and can make your facial expressions look aggressive.

👋
Get Attention Properly

Tap them lightly on the shoulder or give a small wave from a distance. Avoid waving your hands in their face.

🤐
Don't Cover Your Mouth

Many deaf people use lip-reading to supplement signing. Keep your face clear and visible at all times.

💡
Ensure Good Lighting

Sign in well-lit areas. If you're in a dark room, move to where your hands and face are clearly visible.

🤝
Don't Talk to Interpreters

Always look at and talk directly to the deaf person, not the person interpreting for them.

📝
Use Visual Aids

If you're stuck, use a phone's notes app or a pen and paper. Visual communication is always better than no communication.

🏃
Don't Walk Between Signers

If you must pass through two people signing, walk quickly. Don't stop to apologize as it's more distracting.

🗣️
Be Patient & Clear

Speak at a normal pace. If asked to repeat, do so patiently. It takes time for both sides to adjust!

🙈
Don't Assume Lip Reading

Not all deaf people can lip-read perfectly. In fact, only about 30% of English can be understood through lips alone.

🧘
Keep a Clear Background

Avoid busy or cluttered backgrounds. A simple, solid-colored backdrop makes signs much easier to follow.

🙅
Don't Turn Your Back

Never turn your back while someone is signing to you. It's equivalent to walking away while someone is talking.

Did you know?

Facial expressions and body language provide the "tone of voice" in ASL. A raised eyebrow can turn a statement into a question!
